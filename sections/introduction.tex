\section{Introduction}
 
Large language models are increasingly used as sources of current information and have become one of the growing sources for news information and its interpretation, especially among young users \citep{reuters2026dnr, niemanlab2026chatbots}. Despite their issues with hallucinations \citep{huang2025hallucination} and struggles with fast-changing knowledge \citep{vu2024freshllms}, techniques such as RAG have greatly improved their factual grounding and recency abilities \citep{lewis2020rag}. Research shows news integrity of LLM agents can be seriously compromised by sourcing problems and accuracy issues \citep{ebu2025newsintegrity, jazwinska2025citation}. Existing reviews mostly measure accuracy against the auditing outlet's own articles \citep{ebu2025newsintegrity, bbc2025representation} or measure citation retrieval from excerpts \citep{jazwinska2025citation}, and censorship audits often use fixed and historical prompts \citep{naseh2025r1dacted, pan2026censorship}. This project looks at LLM outputs when queried about recent events and compares the depth and coverage of model-generated information against a fact-checked, multi-outlet baseline. As people rely more and more on information catered by LLMs, this comparison would offer a better understanding of how information is mediated, framed or omitted by this channel, and inform users, developers, and regulators of AI-generated news content reliability.
 
Language models usually have a cut-off date for training data and can fail to be up-to-date with the current world \citep{vu2024freshllms}, but web search and retrieval augmentation abilities allow them to answer queries related to recent events. Compared to traditional search engines where links are explicitly displayed and used more often \citep{reuters2026chatbots}, sources in LLM-synthesized information are often opaque or absent \citep{liu2023verifiability}, and users are less likely to visit the linked sources even when they are cited \citep{chapekis2025pew}. This project, therefore, studies LLM responses about recent events and their factual accuracy, depth, and sourcing, and how information present in fact-checked reporting may be omitted, reframed, or replaced. The rising use of AI as a source of information raises issues of public trust and its implication on democratic participation \citep{ebu2025newsintegrity}, with those who are most exposed to AI news also the most likely to trust it \citep{niemanlab2026chatbots}.
 
In this project, we propose a cross-model audit of how five widely used chatbots present \review{[*one to three]} recent news events by comparing model outputs against fact-checked reporting from established outlets. Previous work shows that AI assistants frequently misrepresent and mis-cite news \citep{ebu2025newsintegrity, jazwinska2025citation} and that LLM outputs are less epistemically diverse than a basic web search \citep{wright2025epistemic}. However, many studies mainly measure single metrics using archival or adversarial prompts through APIs or proprietary methods. We need a good understanding of how the factualness, sourcing and framing of information \review{[*check what we are measuring in Methods]} about recent events differ across models and settings to assess whether AI-mediated news narrows or distorts the information environment relative to direct browsing. We will test \review{[*if search-enabled responses are more accurate than search-disabled ones, if citation support is incomplete, and if low-coverage details of more current events are the most vulnerable to low-quality sources \citep{newsguard2025monitor, robotstxt2025misinfo}]}. The results would inform designs that better incorporate uncertainty and source transparency, provide regulators with an empirical foundation for systemic risk assessments and questions of liability for AI-generated claims, and support news literacy guidance on cross-checking AI answers. We will release our code and data.
 
The rest of the proposal will discuss related literature, experimental and evaluation methods, as well as any ethical concerns.
